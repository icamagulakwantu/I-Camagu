# Ceremony order and seating: research review (23 Nov 2026)

Asked by the founder: put every ceremony plan in the order things happen,
make umgidi the last part of ulwaluko, take medical wording out of the
app, and let families seat each group in its own place. The founder's word
decides; sources below were used to check order and to find what was
missing. Online sources are thin and often describe one family, region or
an outsider's visit, so nothing here is treated as a rule for every family.
Ulwaluko is sacred: nothing about what happens on the mountain is added.

## What the founder set
- **Seating (hierarchy).** At umgidi and every ceremony of isiko lakwaXhosa
  (inkomo, ukukhapha, ukubuyisa, intambo enkulu, imbeleko...), each group
  sits in its own place: ootata apart from oomama, abafana apart, and so on.
- **Umgidi is one day,** the day the umkhwetha leaves the mountain as
  ikrwala: the last part of ulwaluko. The mountain part usually lasts about
  a month, sometimes less. The plan must read like the journey.
- **His hair is cut before the ibhoma is built.**
- **No mention of a wound healing or drying,** anywhere.

## What the sources say

### The order of ulwaluko
- Sources agree on the shape: preparation at home, going in (ukungena),
  seclusion (ubukhwetha) with a marker at the end of the first week
  (ukosiswa / uMojiso), coming out (umphumo), umgidi at home, then the
  new man (ubukrwala).
- Preparation at home: his head is shaved before he goes, "to symbolize
  how they are leaving childhood behind" (several accounts; one says by his
  elder brothers). One source says a goat is slaughtered at home when the
  boy goes. Adults build the ibhoma.
- Boys wear the family's ubulunga (inkomo yobulunga, with hair from a
  cow's tail) during ulwaluko (museum record).
- Coming out: a new blanket; the ibhoma and his old things are burnt "on
  the day of umgidi or shortly thereafter"; he is walked home.
- Seclusion length: sources range from one or two weeks to "28 to 30
  days", matching the founder (about a month, sometimes less).

### The umgidi day
- The new man is seated (on an ikhukho) and the men speak to him in turn.
- **Ukuyala:** "the men get the first opportunity to admonish the new man",
  then "the women of the family and the eldest aunt who created the
  necklace (ubulunga)".
- **Gifts (izipho)** and public recognition follow; the family slaughters a
  sheep or cow.
- **Seating:** a Langa account describes two circles of men, one for
  middle-aged men (udodana) and one for young men (isifana); the women stay
  in the house while the men address him. Indlu yesibane is a gathering for
  initiated men only. No source gives a full seating plan: the founder's
  fourteen groups are the basis.

### Other ceremonies
- **Umkhapho / ukukhapha:** the officiator calls on the clan's ancestors by
  name *before* the slaughter; it is not a celebration. **Umbuyiso /
  ukubuyisa** (about a year after burial) is festive, with beer, and the
  dead is called back home by name.
- **Imbeleko:** umqombothi is brewed and imphepho burnt; the elders speak
  to the ancestors for the child; a goat is offered; a strip of its skin
  (isiphandla) is made for the child. The app's order already matched.
- Brewing umqombothi takes days, so it belongs before the ceremony, not at
  its closing.

## What changed in the app
1. **Plans read in the order of the journey,** not the order steps fall
   due. Ulwaluko: registration, preparing him at home (hair), spiritual
   items, building the ibhoma, going to the mountain, daily essentials,
   the first seven days, uMojiso, coming out, ukutshiswa kwebhoma, going
   home, then umgidi (getting ready, then the day in order: he arrives,
   everyone in their place, ukuyala and izipho, closing), then ubukrwala.
   Every ceremony has its own order (`shared/ceremonyPhases.js`).
2. **Umgidi is no longer offered as a separate ceremony to start;** it is
   inside the ulwaluko plan. Umgidi plans made before keep working.
3. **New steps:** a goat at home when he goes in, if the family does this;
   ubulunga, if the family keeps one; everyone in their place before he is
   seated; ukuyala (men first, then the women and the eldest aunt); izipho;
   a seating step in every isiko ceremony.
4. **Seating and groups** (was "Who brings what"): the groups in order of
   seniority, each with where it sits (ootata ebuhlanti, oomama endlini
   enkulu, abafana belali in their own circle, iintombi apart, indlu
   yesibane for initiated men only at umgidi...). Families move groups up
   or down, change places, add or remove groups, and still give each group
   its share.
5. **Wording:** the first-week diet line no longer gives a medical reason
   ("as the ikhankatha guides"); the intambo slaughter step reads plainly.
6. **Order fixes in other ceremonies:** in ukukhapha and ukubuyisa the
   ancestors are addressed before the slaughter; brewing moves to before
   the day in intambo enkulu and inkobe; ukukhapha no longer lists
   umqombothi (its own rule: no fermented drink).
7. Saved plans were brought up to date once (`ceremony-order-nov-2026`),
   keeping every tick and everything already bought.

## The founder's answers (23 Nov 2026)
- **Abayeni** are the men married to the daughters of the house hosting the
  ceremony; **abafazi** are the women married into that house. The seating
  places now say so.
- **Intambo Enkulu** is mainly about being accepted at a higher level: like
  a second imbeleko, after which you may do things you could not before.
  The ceremony card now says so (it wrongly said it binds two families).
- "Demos" was a slip; nothing to add.

## Ukuphuthuma: the stages of the igqirha's calling
The founder asked for the stages to be researched. No source uses the word
"ukuphuthuma", so the founder's name is kept for the ceremony. The stages
follow Mlisa (2009), *Ukuthwasa initiation of amagqirha: identity
construction and the training of Xhosa women as traditional healers*
(University of the Free State), on Xhosa women in the Eastern Cape:

1. The calling is foretold.
2. Signs of the calling (ubizo) appear.
3. A time of intense affliction.
4. Confusion, resistance and/or acceptance.
5. **Ukuvuma ukufa**, accepting the calling: ukungqawa (beads),
   ukungqwamba (goatskins), each with a cleansing (ukuhlamba) and an
   acceptance; ukubekwa ephehlweni, being placed with the teacher. One
   account: two goats, one for the cleansing and one for the ancestors.
6. **Ukuphuma**, coming out: ukutshotsha, umgongqo and ukubaleka.
7. **Ukuphinda indlela**, going back along the path.

Other sources agree on the shape: the calling comes through dreams and
illness; training under an experienced igqirha takes months to years;
imfukamo, a seclusion in a reed hut by the river; intlombe, the singing
and dancing gathering at the heart of every stage; and a coming out with
drums through the night.

In the app the plan has five stages: Ubizo (1-4), Ukuvuma ukufa (5),
Ukuthwasa (the training, with imfukamo), Ukuphuma (6) and Ukuphinda
indlela (7). Each ritual step is named and marked "as the teacher
directs": the app lists what the family provides, never how a step is
done.

Still for the founder: are these the stages your family knows by
ukuphuthuma, and is anything missing or in the wrong order?
