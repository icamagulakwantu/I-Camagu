# Hire for ceremonies, and how stokvels run: research (23 Nov 2026)

Asked by the founder: what families hire for imigidi and other ceremonies at
home in Gqeberha (tents, chairs, pots, stoves, fridges), with real prices for
planning; and worked examples of a fully used drinks stokvel, grocery stokvel,
society, and a fully prepared ulwaluko-to-umgidi plan. The stokvel rules are
the founder's own; the prices below are dated and sourced.

## The examples (founder's rules)
- **Umgidi drinks stokvel:** 50 members, R1,500 a month for 10 months
  (R15,000 each), each taking home 3 cases of Commando brandy, 3 of Old Buck
  gin, 10 of beer and 10 of cider. At i-Camagu bulk prices on 23 Nov 2026 the
  basket is R18,850, so saving alone falls R3,850 short: the interest the
  members raise closes the gap. Bought together: 150 + 150 + 500 + 500 cases.
- **Grocery stokvel:** R350 a month (R250 fridge, R100 dry) from January to
  the end of November, 15 members. Each may lend what they have paid in less
  a R100 retainer, at 30%; the interest returns to the stokvel, covers bad
  debts and grows the buying money; each member has a target and pays any
  shortfall themselves; interest is shared equally. At year end groceries
  are bought together and the rest is shared for school uniform and
  stationery, with receipts.
- **Society:** money only, from R100 a month with no maximum; each member
  raises their own interest and takes home their own money and interest.
- **Ulwaluko to umgidi:** the real ulwaluko plan for 150 guests, the day
  before umgidi, with the hire plan, seating and the drinks.
- The interest targets (R4,000 drinks, R1,000 grocery) are examples; each
  group sets its own.

## A caution for the founder
A law firm's guide to stokvels and the National Credit Regulator notes that a
stokvel lending at interest to people who are not members can fall under the
National Credit Act. The examples say so, plainly, so groups can check before
they lend. (Cliffe Dekker Hofmeyr, Stokvels and the National Credit
Regulator.)
