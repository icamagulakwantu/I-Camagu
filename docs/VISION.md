# i-Camagu: what it is and what it stands for

Told by the founder, 20 November 2026, and written down here so that every
change to the app (design, wording, which features come first, what we
leave out) can be checked against it. Draft: the founder corrects it before
it is final.

## In one line

i-Camagu is the infrastructure of the Xhosa nation: the roads and bridges
that let Xhosa people find each other, keep their ways, and grow rich
together, without having to force unity by themselves. All they have to do
is take part.

## Why it exists

Xhosa people, mostly in the Eastern Cape and the Western Cape, have been
left behind in development, in education and in the stability of the
region. An economy needs more than a port and mines; it needs roads,
bridges and places to fill up with petrol. A nation is the same.
i-Camagu is that infrastructure, so the Xhosa nation can become
prosperous and have a footprint in the world, as the Seminole tribes and
the Jewish community have built for their own people.

It is not just an app. It is a platform where Xhosa people plan and
preserve their ceremonies, learn and respect their ways, find each other,
and come together to build.

## What unites us, from the root up

1. **Isiduko.** How we identify each other and who we are: deeper than a
   name and surname, it is your root. It is why you cannot marry someone
   who shares your isiduko. Everything in the Xhosa nation is rooted in it.
   Each clan has a male and a female form of the same name: AmaTshawe and
   Mam-Tshawe, AmaCirha and MamCirha, AmaCwerha and MamCwerha, AmaZangwa and
   MamZangwa. Same clan, man and woman.

2. **Unwavering belief in our ancestors**, lived through ceremonies all our
   lives, yearly and monthly, through and through:
   - *Birth and belonging.* Imbeleko introduces a child to the ancestors
     where they are born, fed and raised. Intambo enkulu takes them to the
     next level within that clan. A child belongs to the mother's side
     unless the father comes, asks her ancestors respectfully and pays for
     having raised the child; then the child takes his clan name. A child
     of a single mother belongs entirely to her side.
   - *Becoming.* Ulwaluko and umgidi; intonjane.
   - *Passing on.* Ukukhapha. A man is sent and then brought back
     (ukubuyiswa), because he is expected to protect the home: spiritually
     he must be alive in it. A woman is sent through inkobe zikamama, to
     guide from the next realm together with the father of the house, and
     to connect the family to God.
   - *Amagqirha.* People who live in this world and also cross into the
     spiritual realm; they heal and protect the nation. Their trainees are
     amakhwetha/amathwasa. Their calling has its own ceremonies, at the
     sea, the dam or the bush, where the ancestors are spoken to.

3. **Saving together.** Stokvels, burial societies, grocery stokvels and
   alcohol stokvels are the way of the people in the Eastern Cape. They save
   so they can buy in bulk, but they do not reach their full buying power,
   because nothing brings them together in large numbers. i-Camagu does.

4. **Memory.** Ukugida: the books, kept for years, of who gave what at whose
   umgidi, wedding or ceremony. Those books get lost. i-Camagu keeps them,
   and keeps the family archive with them.

## One vehicle

i-Camagu has many moving parts but is one vehicle. None works properly
without the others. (Proposed mapping, for the founder to confirm.)

| Part of the vehicle | Part of i-Camagu | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Chassis | **Isiduko** | Everything is built on who you are. |
| Engine | **Ceremonies** | Belief in the ancestors is what drives us, all our lives. |
| Steering wheel | **Learning our ways** | The rites, their meaning, our history and heroes keep us on our road. |
| Gears | **Stokvels** | Saving together turns small amounts into power. |
| Tyres | **Bulk buying (SmartMatch)** | Where that power meets the road: buying together at scale. |
| Logbook | **Ukugida and the family archive** | The record of the journey, never lost. |
| Fuel | **The Shrine Fund** | Money we keep in our own communities, invested in what we own. |

The bottom bar is Home · Ceremonies · Bulk Buy · Stokvels · Iziduko · More. Bulk Buy
is also how the platform earns its keep and sustains itself. Ukugida is a
feature inside a ceremony, not a ceremony or a section of its own: it lives
on each ceremony's page (and in More). The clan is the first card on Home
and the first tile in More.

## The two matches: the core of the engine

- **ClanMatch** finds your people through isiduko and umnombo.
- **SmartMatch** finds your buying partners. It takes what families and
  stokvels buy, groups the same things across many orders (someone wants 3
  cases of Hennessy, someone 1, someone 2, forty people in Gqeberha), places
  it as one big order at the volume price that retailers, wholesalers and
  distributors give for pallets, then divides it and delivers it or has it
  collected at a central place. Stokvels already buy in bulk on their own;
  i-Camagu pulls many stokvels together into one far bigger order. This is
  also how the platform earns: the negotiated volume discount.

## The Shrine Fund (ubuhlanti)

The Shrine is owned by the people at large and designed to advance the
Xhosa nation. Some things it owns outright. In businesses it funds, it holds
an agreed percentage and earns a return on its investment; not every
business it funds is run by the community, and the share is set deal by
deal, in open books.

## Ceremony food

In the Eastern Cape the ceremony starch is white samp, not pap.

## What this means for every change

- **Easy first.** Many parts, one simple experience. A person new to apps
  must understand what to do next on every screen.
- **Show the vehicle.** Each part should lead to the others: a ceremony
  shows its saving, its buying, the clan who come, and its ukugida book;
  a stokvel shows what it is saving for.
- **Isiduko is the root,** not an extra.
- **Respect before features.** Sacred matters (ulwaluko, the amagqirha's
  calling) are named with care and never over-explained.
- **Truthful.** No fake listings, figures or claims; history stated as it
  can be defended.
- **Calm.** Cream, deep green for structure, one accent for the main action.

## Ceremonies follow the journey (founder, 23 Nov 2026)

- A plan reads in the order things happen. Ulwaluko runs from home (his
  hair is cut before the ibhoma is built) to the mountain, coming out, and
  umgidi: one day, the day he comes home as ikrwala, the last part of the
  journey. Umgidi is planned inside ulwaluko, not as a ceremony of its own.
- At umgidi and every ceremony of isiko lakwaXhosa each group sits in its
  own place: ootata apart from oomama, abafana apart, and so on (Seating
  and groups).
- Nothing in the app describes a wound healing or drying.
- See docs/research/2026-11-ceremony-order-and-seating.md.

## Open questions

- Stokvel history: the common account is that the word comes from the
  "stock fairs" (cattle auctions) of English settler farmers in the Eastern
  Cape in the early 1800s, which Black people, largely Xhosa, attended and
  carried into the savings clubs. How should i-Camagu tell it?
- The igqirha's work is **ukuphuthuma** (founder, 22 Nov 2026), with stages
  as the person is introduced into the calling. Still to record: the stages,
  in order, and what each needs.
- The father's claiming of a child (asking the mother's ancestors and
  paying): its name, and whether it should be a ceremony in the app.
